# Central pawn tension — what each resolution does (the teaching content)

Sources: Watson *Secrets of Modern Chess Strategy*; Kmoch *Pawn Power in
Chess* (the "lever"); Nimzowitsch *My System* (restraint/blockade); Silman
*How to Reassess Your Chess*; Heisman (the "unnecessary release of central
tension" as a hallmark of weaker play).

**The situation:** two central pawns in diagonal capture contact — e.g.
White d4 vs Black e5 (dxe5 / exd4 both legal), or White e4 vs Black d5.
Kmoch calls this cocked lever a source of latent energy. Three ways to
discharge it, each with a definite strategic consequence a student should
be told:

## 1. KEEP the tension (maintain)
- Retains MAXIMUM flexibility: you keep BOTH capture options *and* the
  push option; the structure is still undetermined.
- Forces the opponent to keep accounting for several possible pawn
  structures at once — they must be ready for all of them.
- Principle: the side better placed to benefit from *any* resolution
  should hold the tension and improve pieces first; whoever resolves first
  usually clarifies things *for the opponent*.
- The mark of stronger play. Cost: a piece can get tied to defending the
  tension, and the opponent may get to resolve it on their terms.

## 2. LOCK the center (push past — e.g. d4-d5)
- FIXES the structure permanently → this is the move that DEFINES the
  plans for both sides. The position stops being fluid.
- Gains space, but concedes the square the pushing pawn used to guard:
  d4-d5 gives up control of e5 and c5, handing the opponent outpost
  squares there — while simultaneously creating outposts for YOU further
  up (d5 supports c6/e6; the closed center makes f5/b5 usable and safe).
- Shifts the game to the WINGS: "when the centre is closed, play on the
  flank." The classic Ruy Lopez: d5 closes the centre → White maneuvers
  a knight to f5 (Nf1-e3-f5 / Nh2-g4-f5), Black plays ...f5 or expands on
  the queenside.
- Commit only when the resulting fixed structure favors you.
- COMPOSES with the strong-square knight outpost: locking the centre is
  *what makes* the wing outpost permanent. lock -> outpost is one campaign.

## 3. RESOLVE immediately (capture — dxe5)
- Simplest and most concrete — but usually EASES the opponent's game: it
  clarifies the structure and typically opens a file / improves a piece
  *for them* (the recapture develops a piece or seizes a file).
- Surrenders the flexibility of (1) for no structural gain unless the
  capture concretely wins something: a file you will actually control, a
  tempo, or fixing an enemy pawn as a target.
- The classic sub-1500 error (Heisman): releasing central tension to
  "resolve the uncertainty" — a psychological relief that hands the
  stronger structural chances to the opponent. Correct only with a
  concrete follow-up, not as a default.

## How we surface it (design)
- The THREE explanations above are STATIC literature — always shown when
  central tension exists (they don't depend on the position).
- WHICH mode is right HERE is engine/Maia-VERIFIED per position: detect in
  the banked lines whether best play keeps (no central pawn move), locks
  (central push) or resolves (central capture), and surface that as the
  recommendation alongside the three explanations.
- Caveat on the sub-1500 hypothesis: we only have Maia-2400 banked (strong
  humans), not Maia-1500, so we can measure how often STRONG play resolves
  vs keeps/locks, but we cannot directly contrast weak-vs-strong. A low
  strong-play resolve rate is consistent with (not proof of) the "resolve
  = weak" claim.
